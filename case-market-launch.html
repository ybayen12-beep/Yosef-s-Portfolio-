I led a new U.S. state market's catalogue launch straight into production

A new regulated U.S. state market needed its full catalogue of games deployed to production, with tracking records, before go-live. There was no test copy of the system to try it on first.

The situation

This launch had no place to rehearse. The market had no staging environment, which is a test copy of the system where teams try changes before they go live. Every deployment went straight to production, the live system.

The work also had to be tracked from start to finish. Each game needed a tracking record before go-live.

My role

I was the Project Manager and launch lead. I built the ticket and tracking structure for the market and set the naming and sourcing rules the work followed. I also planned the engineers' deployment work and created the activation tickets.

What I did

  1. I built the ticket and tracking structure for the market and set the rules for how tickets were named and sourced.
  2. I created 65 production deployment tickets: 63 new and 2 reused.
  3. I created 65 tracking records.
  4. I scheduled the deployment tickets across 4 engineers over 5 working days.
  5. When the go-live date moved, I updated the due dates on all the tracking records and verified each one.
  6. I created 31 activation tickets in one batch.
  7. Later, I built 4 standalone scripts, small programs that each run on their own, to run the same steps for this market.

The result

  • 65 production deployment tickets were in place, 63 new and 2 reused, scheduled across 4 engineers over 5 working days.
  • All 65 tracking records carried updated, verified due dates after the go-live date moved.
  • 31 activation tickets were created in one batch.
  • The same steps now exist as 4 scripts for this market.
  • My scope grew from five U.S. markets plus one Canadian province to six U.S. markets plus the province.
