The situation

Every installation depended on several handoffs lining up on the same day: equipment leaving the factory, materials arriving on site, engineers traveling in, the site being ready, and building security letting the crew through.

One missed handoff could stall an install, leaving engineers on site with nothing they could do.

What I owned

I owned the coordination behind each installation, from design through deployment. I was the single point of contact between our factories, the field engineers, the client's facilities team and building security, and I planned and scheduled the work with the engineering team.

The decision: confirm readiness before committing

I confirmed each site's readiness before releasing equipment or booking engineer travel, rather than shipping and flying crews on the original date and hoping the site was ready. If a dependency was open, the date moved before anyone traveled, not after they arrived.

How I ran it

  1. Site intake. For each install I gathered the site contacts, access rules, delivery details and install requirements, and sent the site a readiness checklist.
  2. Dependency tracking. I tracked what each site still needed before install day, such as power, mounting and network, and followed up until each item was closed.
  3. Factory-to-site logistics. I coordinated with our factories on ship dates, tracked shipments, and scheduled delivery and receiving at each site location.
  4. Field engineer deployment. I booked engineers' flights and travel and gave them what they needed on arrival: the site address, contacts and access instructions.
  5. Security access. I arranged building security clearance for the crew at each site, including badge requests and any access-hour approvals the building required.
  6. Inventory and logistics. I ran the warehouse and device logistics, tracking inventory in Excel and Microsoft Project so equipment was accounted for from factory to site.

Risks I managed

  • Late shipments. Tracking ship dates against the install date showed a delay early enough to adjust the plan.
  • Sites not ready. The readiness checklist and dependency follow-up kept crews from arriving to an unprepared site.
  • Access problems. Clearing security before travel kept engineers from being held at the door.

The result

  • Installations for high-profile clients, including structured cabling, video display systems and technical integrations, coordinated from design through deployment.
  • Work carried out to client specifications and safety standards.
