The situation: root cause analysis

The tracking board looked current, but it was not. The automation that filled it set each item's status once, when the item was created, and never refreshed it. Old statuses showed as if they were current.

I had found a related problem in August 2026. When a game was reclassified as an exclusive deal, its original tracking record stayed open forever. The same delivery showed up twice.

What I owned: the data quality audit

I owned this audit, from the first question to the last fix. I raised that question myself in September 2026: did the automation keep the board accurate?

The decision: scope definition

I checked the whole board rather than a sample. That meant every item and every smaller task in all nine sections, each compared against the current status of its ticket.

For duplicates, I tested the detection rule against every current record rather than a few hand-picked examples, before removing anything.

How I ran it: change approval and quality control

  • I set one rule for every change: no change without approval.
  • I sequenced the work: compare, fix with approval, review my own fixes, then re-check the live ticket status.
  • I required a second review of my own fixes. It caught 114 wrong updates of mine, and I undid every one.
  • On the re-check, I moved 57 more items up to the status they had reached and gave 77 items with no date the correct label.
  • I traced the duplicates to the automation that keeps the board in step with the tickets. I then wrote a rule to spot them, meaning a check that flags two records for the same delivery.

The result

  • 699 status fixes and 6 section moves are in place and checked.
  • 114 of my own wrong updates were found and undone.
  • 57 more items moved up to their correct status, and 77 items with no date were re-labelled correctly.
  • The duplicate check covered 2,130 records and found 29 duplicates, with 0 records flagged by mistake.
  • With approval, I cancelled 25 duplicate records, plus 37 child records (smaller records attached to a main one) and 3 leftover tickets with nothing linked to them.
  • These are counts of fixes and checks. I did not measure what changed for the people using the board.
